# Statecraft 3.0 — Engagement and Retention

Where the excitement comes from, and why a player comes back tomorrow. Every item here must be honest: no fake urgency, no paid power, no dark patterns.

## 1. Inside a round (seconds)

| Moment | What the player feels | How the design makes it happen |
|---|---|---|
| Typing an order | "The game understood me" | The card appears within about a second, with the player's own numbers |
| Forecast on the card | "My decision has weight" | Treasury, prices and trust move as fields change |
| A notice from another leader | "Someone is reacting to me" | Inbox badge, short sound, the country pulses on the globe |
| The timer ring | Pressure, but fair | Planning has a fixed length; a soft warning at 15 seconds |
| Lock | Commitment | Cards flip face down; nobody can change them now |

## 2. The reveal (the biggest moment)

Resolution is a short show, not a table:

1. The globe turns to each event in order of cause.
2. Arcs cut, ships reroute, prices tick on the world market strip.
3. The player's own stats count up or down with the cause chain shown beside each change.
4. The surprise line: *"Forecast: oil +4%. Actual: oil +11% — Russia also cut exports to China."*

The gap between forecast and actual is the hook. It turns other players into the story.

## 3. Across a match (minutes)

- **Threads:** a negotiation that runs over three rounds is a small story with an ending.
- **Reliability:** players watch who keeps deals. Trust becomes something you build and spend.
- **Comebacks:** score is improvement from your own start (2.0), so a small country can still win the match.
- **Headlines:** each round ends with one World Wire headline about the biggest event. Players want their move to be the headline.

## 4. After a match (the reason to replay)

- **Chronicle card:** a shareable front page built from the event log, with the player's own quoted words. *"THE ENERGY REALIGNMENT — India 'stopped depending on one supplier'."*
- **What if?:** pick one past order, flip it, and see a quick re-simulation from that round with bots playing the others. Unranked, clearly labelled. This is the main replay loop.
- **Rating change:** the leader rating moves with a short animation; the next title is shown with progress.
- **Wish list feedback:** if the player tried an unsupported idea and it is added later, they get a notice. Their idea changed the game.

## 5. Across days

- **Seeded worlds:** every match starts from a different seed (prices, droughts, tensions), so the same four countries tell a new story.
- **Weekly world:** one shared seed per week for a public leaderboard, so friends can compare the same starting world.
- **Friends' rooms:** Create room and Join with code (D-9) remain the strongest reason to return.

## 6. Guard rails

- No streak punishment, no loot boxes, no countdown sales.
- Match length stays 20–25 minutes; the game says when a match will end.
- Free text never gives an advantage over chips: the card is the same.
- Monetization follows 2.0 D-1: depth, comfort and style only.

## 7. Metrics for the slice playtest

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Players who use free text at least once per match | > 60% |
| Free-text orders confirmed without edits | > 70% (interpreter accuracy) |
| Proposals answered with Counter at least once per match | > 40% of players |
| Players who open What if? after a match | > 30% |
| Players who start a second match the same day | > 35% |
